Kerala Crop Price Forecaster

Live · Telegram bot

Same day, same banana, seven Kerala markets: ₹51/kg in one, ₹70/kg in another. The prices are published daily on Agmarknet — in English, hard to search, no outlook, no alerts — so most farmers go by what the middleman tells them. This bot does the checking for them in Malayalam: today's price in every market for 17 crops, the range to expect over the next week, and a message when a crop crosses the price you named. It refreshes itself every morning.

What the data said LightGBM trained on 750,000 prices couldn't beat "tomorrow's price equals today's" at 1–7 days. So I shipped that naive guess with an honest range instead, and the model only takes over at the horizons where it has proven it wins.

Agentic Data Intelligence Platform

Nine agents, one gate

Nine agents carry raw business data from upload to decision — ingesting CSV, Excel, SQL and REST behind one data contract, learning what normal looks like, then catching schema drift, null floods and distribution shifts in every new file. Safe, reversible problems get repaired automatically, each repair re-validated and reverted byte-for-byte if it didn't work. Anything consequential stops and asks a human, evidence attached.

The architecture separates reasoning from authority: detection is deterministic, diagnosis is one narrowly scoped model call under a closed schema, and action is deterministic again behind a rule-derived gate — so a wrong or even malicious model response has no path to your data.

What the data said I logged the model's self-reported confidence across live runs. 82% came back at exactly 0.95, inside a band 0.08 wide. The confidence threshold was doing no routing work at all — the deterministic layers had been carrying it the whole time.

Kerala Flood Risk Intelligence

Satellite + terrain

Answers one question: if I buy this property in Kerala, will it flood? Not by predicting rain, but by reading terrain — elevation, slope, distance to water, catchment area and historical paddy land, drawn from Copernicus DEM, Sentinel-1, Dynamic World and 159,168 OpenStreetMap waterway features. A Random Forest scores it, 12 physics-based rules constrain it where pure ML fails on geographic edge cases, and SHAP explains every single prediction.

How I work

Three habits that show up in every project above.

01

Measure before optimising

A chain taking 28 seconds turned out to be spending 2.3 of them per call on a DNS fallback, not on the model. Profiling the transport beat tuning the thing everyone assumes is slow.

02

Falsify your own guarantees

Every safety claim in the platform has a script that removes the protection and proves the test catches it. A guarantee nobody has tried to break is just a guess.

03

Report what failed

The model that lost to a naive baseline. The confidence score that meant nothing. The passing test that asserted nothing. They're in the write-ups too, because they tell you whether the rest is true.
